## 2. Questions for the lawyer

One hour, in this order. Bring: a rendered share card, a screenshot of the app, the pricing page,
and this file.

**Framing sentence to open with:** *"I have a paid web app that reads a user's fantasy football
league from Sleeper's and ESPN's public endpoints, computes recommendations, and produces a
shareable image with NFL player names, statistics and headshots on it. I charge $3 to $7 for the
season. I want to know what I can't do."*

### On player likeness and imagery (the expensive one)
1. **Headshots.** I fetch NFL player photographs from Sleeper's and ESPN's public CDNs and put
   them on a card that promotes a paid product. What is my exposure on copyright in the photo, and
   on right of publicity for the player? Does it change if the card is shared publicly versus shown
   only inside the app?
2. **Names and stats.** My understanding is that *CBC Distribution v. MLB Advanced Media* protects
   the use of player names and statistics by a fantasy operator. Does that hold for what I am
   doing, and does it extend to putting a name on a promotional image?
3. **Team logos and abbreviations.** I render NFL team marks next to players. Nominative fair use,
   or a licence I do not have?
4. **If the answer to 1 is "don't"**, is initials-only (which I have already built) sufficient, or
   does the player's *name* on a promotional card carry the same problem?

### On platform terms
5. **Sleeper.** Their docs say the API is free for non-commercial use and that commercial use needs
   a licensing conversation. If they never reply to my email, where does that leave me? Is
   "we asked and heard nothing" a defence or an aggravation?
6. **ESPN.** I read an undocumented endpoint on `lm-api-reads.fantasy.espn.com`. What is the
   realistic exposure — terms of use, CFAA, something else — for read-only access to leagues their
   own users have made public?
7. **User-authorised access.** If I later support private ESPN leagues by having the user give me
   their `espn_s2`/`SWID` cookies, what changes? This is the feature my users ask for most and the
   one I am most nervous about.

### On the business
8. **Entity.** Single-member LLC in my state versus operating as a sole trader, for a business
   that might make a few thousand dollars and has the liability profile above. Worth it?
9. **The advice disclaimer.** My terms say the recommendations are projections, that I publish my
   error rate, and that I am not liable for a lost league. Is that disclaimer doing the work I
   think it is?
10. **Accuracy claims.** I intend to publicly advertise measured hit rates ("Lock calls are right
    about 80% of the time"). What do I need to be able to show if someone challenges that?
11. **AI-written explanations.** Some user-facing text is generated by a language model from my
    own numbers. Do I have to disclose that, and does it change my liability for what it says?
12. **Refunds.** Seven days, no questions, on a $7 product. Anything wrong with that, and does it
    need to be in the terms or just published?

### Cheap and fast
13. Is there anything on my published pages that is actively wrong or that I should delete?
14. What is the one thing I have not asked about that you would worry about?

## 3. What the drafts already say

The three pages were written against the code, not from a template, so they are accurate today
and will go stale the moment the product changes. What they commit us to:

**Terms** (`/legal/terms`)
- Season passes, one-time, no renewal, ending with the season.
- Recommendations are projections with published confidence; no guarantee; not betting advice.
- User is responsible for their own lineup decisions.
- No scraping, no resale, no automated volume.
- Explicit non-affiliation with the NFL, NFLPA, Sleeper, ESPN and Yahoo.
- AI writes some explanations from our numbers; it never decides or invents a number.
- No uptime guarantee, but we make good on an outage that costs a paid week.

**Privacy** (`/legal/privacy`)
- Exactly what we store, matching `docs/DATA_INVENTORY.md` field for field.
- No passwords, no card numbers, no advertising identifiers, no selling data.
- Named sub-processors: Sleeper, ESPN, Stripe, Supabase, Anthropic, our hosts.
- Explicit that the Claude API receives player names and engine numbers, but not the user's email
  or league id. This is true — see `edge/engine/explain.py:verdict_payload`.
- Export and deletion, with the honest warning that deletion revokes the season pass.
- Retention: recommendation logs through the following season, then deleted.

**Refunds** (`/legal/refunds`)
- Seven days, no questions, email from the purchase address.
- Refund removes the pass; the account survives.
- Later than seven days still gets a look if the product was broken.

## 4. Review triggers

Re-read these pages when any of the following happens, in the same commit as the change:

- A new field is stored about a user, or a new third party receives data.
- A new SKU, a price change, or any change to what a pass includes.
- Private-league support ships (user credentials change the privacy picture materially).
- A new platform connector ships (Yahoo).
- The projection vendor changes.
- Anything new appears on a public share card.

`docs/DATA_INVENTORY.md` is the source of truth for the privacy page. If the two disagree, the
inventory is right and the page is out of date.
